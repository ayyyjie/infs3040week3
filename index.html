The accounting equation

Everything downstream — SAP, T-accounts, reconciliation accounts — is scaffolding built on top of this one line.

Assets = Liabilities + Owners' equity

Read it as two views of the same company: the left side is what it owns. The right side is how that ownership was financed — either by owing someone else money, or by the owners' own stake. The equation must always balance, transaction by transaction. That balancing act is what "double-entry" means.

Because assets sit on the opposite side of the equation from liabilities and equity, they behave oppositely when it comes to debits and credits — that's the whole source of confusion in Week 3, and it's solved in the next tab.

Paying by card: Bank (an asset) goes down → credit. Supplies (an asset) goes up → debit. Two assets, opposite directions, equation stays balanced.
Rule of thumb: Assets → debit increases, credit decreases. Liabilities & equity → credit increases, debit decreases. Derive it, don't memorise it: assets sit opposite liabilities/equity in the equation, so they move opposite ways.
